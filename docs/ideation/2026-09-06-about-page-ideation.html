An About page for Ardoise — what it is and who it's for

Date 2026-09-06 Topic about-page Mode repo-grounded

Direction under exploration: an « À propos » page for the Ardoise web app that explains to a visitor who knows nothing what the app does and what it's for. These are ranked candidate directions for that page — not a spec. Choose one (or a combination) to brainstorm next.

▸ Grounding Context — Codebase

Ardoise is « expenses, services and paybacks between friends, organised in groups » (CONTEXT.md first line; same in README). Friends keep one shared running slate per Group: a Member records an Expense (real money paid, split into equal Parts) or a Service (an act valued in cents — time, skill); the app derives each Member's Balance; Paybacks settle who owes whom. The value proposition is a behaviour: no mental ledger — the slate always shows who owes whom, and Balances never cross Groups.

The web app is a client-side Hono router (apps/web/src/lib/router.tsx): routes are groups, group, invite, profile, auth — there is no landing, About, help, or intro surface of any kind. A visitor who is not signed in is sent to the bare sign-up/sign-in card on every route except invite (main.tsx: session === null ? <AuthPage />), and the signed-out shell renders only a brand <h1>Ardoise</h1> over that card (AppLayout.tsx: showShell = session !== null && currentRoute !== 'invite'). The invite landing (InvitePage.tsx) — the only acquisition path — shows the Group name and member count, then Rejoindre le Groupe or the auth forms, with zero product explanation.

Vocabulary is authoritative in CONTEXT.md: Ardoise is the product name (not a domain object); it means the French bar tab / slate — a shared running bill at the counter. UI copy is French (sole carve-out; identifiers are English), in a compact, imperative register with capitalized domain nouns (« Mes groupes », « Rejoindre le Groupe », « Aucune Dépense »). The app is invite-only, no user directory, memberships created by opening a secret invite (ADR 0003); Balances are sealed per Group (ADR 0001); French is the single copy language (ADR 0004). No end-user positioning copy exists anywhere in the product — only the README tagline, the page <title>, and the brand heading. Target user: friends who share spending (dinners, trips, shared purchases, evenings) and settle up.

▸ Topic Axes

  • A Core narrative & content — what the page must explain: the shared-slate metaphor, the Expense → Service → Payback → derived Balance mechanics in a Group, the value proposition — in plain French.
  • B Entry & placement — route + link placement; whether the bare logged-out auth card (the current front door) can reach the page; entry points for a person who knows nothing.
  • C Form & depth — static prose vs a visual/demonstrated walkthrough vs guided or expanding sections; single screen vs several sections.
  • D Register, boundaries & trust — tone consistent with the compact French copy and capitalized vocabulary; invite-only/privacy positioning; what not to overclaim.

▸ Ranked Ideas

1. Public About, pitch at the door 2. Show a Slate 3. The « ardoise » delta line 4. Teach the decision (mini-lexicon) 5. One narrated evening 6. Invite landing teaches the instance 7. Honest boundaries + register
1.

Public About, reachable before sign-up — pitch lives at the front door

Axis B

Confidence 88% · Complexity Low

Today an About page is structurally dead for its only real audience: every logged-out route except invite dispatches to the bare auth card. Make /about an open route (add about to the Route union, carve it out of the session === null ? <AuthPage /> gate in main.tsx), render it in a minimal public shell, and put a 2–3 line French pitch + a « Qu'est-ce qu'Ardoise ? » link on the auth card and on /. The enabling routing change also buys a reusable capability: future landing/help/legal pages slot in as one-line entries.

Basis
direct: verified — main.tsx routes non-invite logged-out visitors to AuthPage; router.tsx union has no about member and notFound → groupsRoute; AppLayout.tsx showShell is false signed-out, so nav destinations exist only in the signed-in shell.
Rationale
Every other idea on this page is worthless if the page can't be reached pre-commit; the single most information-hungry visitor is exactly whom the current gate locks out. This is the enabling move.
Downsides
A public (unauthenticated) route and a public shell are a real routing/gating decision with security surface (what stays behind the session wall); needs an explicit navigation-architecture ruling.
2.

Show a Slate — don't describe it

Axis C

Confidence 85% · Complexity Medium

The product's whole promise is that Balances are derived — nobody computes or types a solde. That property can only be felt by watching a small fake Group (fake members, fake money, clearly signposted) rendered with the app's own components — BalanceHero, member chips, the three slate lists — as one Dépense is added, its Parts split, the « On te doit / Tu dois » figures visibly move, and a Règlement clears a line. Playable, or replaying; mounted on About, and (reused as one component) on a brand-new empty Group where the teaching moment actually happens.

Basis
direct: CONTEXT.md — Expense is money that left a pocket split into Parts; Balance « Never typed in »; Payback is real money reducing what is owed. The real components (BalanceHero.tsx « Ton solde / On te doit / Tu dois », the three empty-state lists) already render this model — verified in source.
Rationale
The one thing that cannot be conveyed in prose is precisely the product's differentiator (derivation); and a paragraph asserting it is strictly weaker than a 10-second watch. Building from existing components keeps it register-perfect and cheap.
Downsides
Most expensive option (demo state/components to keep in sync); a maintained static page is the cheap baseline. If unused, it's the biggest single cost.
3.

Open on the « ardoise » delta line — found the French voice

Axis A

Confidence 82% · Complexity Low

A French visitor already knows what an ardoise is (the café slate), so the page must not define the name — it opens on the delta, the one thing a café slate can't do: compute itself. Candidate line in the app's register: « L'ardoise du comptoir se tient au crayon. Celle-ci se calcule toute seule. » No French product sentence exists anywhere today (the CONTEXT/README line is English; the UI shows only the bare brand heading). This line seeds the brand's French voice and is small enough to double as the auth-card subtitle and the page <title> treatment.

Basis
direct: verified — no French intro/positioning copy in the codebase; AppLayout.tsx logged-out branch renders only <h1>Ardoise</h1>; CONTEXT.md's tagline is English-only; the app's own name supplies the built-in contrast.
Rationale
Every strong product page starts from one true sentence, and none exists in French; the name itself carries the metaphor, so generic « track shared expenses » copy would waste it.
Downsides
A voice line is only as good as the page it anchors; on its own it's copy, not a page — needs a content body (ideas 4/5) to live in.
4.

Teach the decision: an on-screen mini-lexicon

Axis A

Confidence 78% · Complexity Low

The real learning cost of this app is the domain model, not the mechanics: Expense vs Service is a genuinely non-obvious distinction (the codebase itself needed ADR 0002), and mis-recording one silently relabels real money as goodwill. Build the body as compact definition rows for the capitalized French nouns the UI already speaks — Dépense / Service / Règlement and the derived Solde — each with a one-sentence contrast and a one-line mini-slate example, mirroring the on-screen tabs and BalanceHero labels so the page doubles as a self-documenting map of the real screens.

Basis
direct: ADR 0002 (Service is not an Expense); CONTEXT.md — Service « not for money spent », Balance « Never typed in »; on-screen nouns from the empty states (Aucune Dépense, Aucun Règlement) and the tabs.
Rationale
Teach the nouns and the rest of the UI teaches itself — the cheapest possible onboarding win that also protects data quality (correct recording). Coupling the sections to live screen labels stops the page silently drifting from the product.
Downsides
Risk of reading like a spec/glossary; must stay scenario-led, not taxonomic, or it loses the casual invitee.
5.

One narrated evening, Balance-first

Axis A

Confidence 74% · Complexity Low

Rather than a glossary, tell one canonical evening — « Vendredi, Léa paie le dîner 84,50 € » — with honest arithmetic (equal Parts, leftover cents to the Payer) and lead with the derived Solde, the thing nobody types and the first thing an invitee meets on the group screen. Bob returns the favour with a Service; the Balance says who owes whom; a Règlement settles it. The story doubles as a glossary-by-encounter for the reader who skims.

Basis
direct: CONTEXT.md — Part « Always an equal split; leftover cents go to the Payer », Balance « Never typed in », Slate « A Member sees a Slate; nobody types one in »; a new user's first real surface is the BalanceHero-led group screen.
Rationale
What makes Ardoise worth explaining is not the input model but that Balances appear by themselves; leading with the derived Solde matches both the product's magic and the visitor's actual first screen.
Downsides
More prose to maintain; a scenario's appeal is subjective. Ideas 4 and 5 are the two competing content-philosophies for the body — this is the key content decision to make when brainstorming.
6.

The invite landing teaches the instance you're joining

Axis B

Confidence 80% · Complexity Low–Medium

The invite screen is the only organic acquisition path and the single highest-intent stranger surface that exists. A stranger's decision to join is made in seconds there, where today nothing explains what they're joining. Instantiate the explanation from data that already exists at invite time: « Bob vous invite sur l'ardoise de ses potes — une note commune, chacun sait qui doit quoi » with the real Group name above Rejoindre le Groupe, plus a link to About. Replace the category lesson (« what is ardoise ») with the instance lesson (« here is our shared slate »), which is the framing that makes a stranger click.

Basis
direct: verified — InvitePage.tsx renders only preview.name and memberCountLabel before the join/sign-up wall; CONTEXT.md — Invite is a stable secret link any Member copies; ADR 0003 (no directory — invites are the only on-ramp).
Rationale
Answers the only question that matters to that visitor (« what is this specific group and why should I be in it ») at the exact moment of decision, and is automatable over live data rather than hand-authored.
Downsides
Partially an expansion beyond the About route itself (it touches the invite surface); scope must be confirmed. Done naively it could read as clutter above a form the visitor already intends to use.
7.

Honest boundaries + ratified register — « ce que ce n'est pas »

Axis D

Confidence 84% · Complexity Low

For a product where real money changes hands among friends, trust is precision, not enthusiasm. A short section states what is true by construction: each Groupe is a separate room (Balances never cross a Group), you enter only by a Member's secret invite (no directory), and Ardoise records and computes but holds and moves no money — plus a register ruling that glossary avoid-lists constrain English identifiers, not French display copy. Writing this page forces that ruling into the open and lets it seed a single French copy source; the observed drift (Créer un User beside Créer le Groupe) then gets fixed at its root.

Basis
direct: ADR 0003 (no user directory / friend graph); ADR 0001 (Balances do not cross Groups); CONTEXT.md — Payback is « Real money changing hands » happening between friends in real life; avoid-lists ban notes de frais / compte / debt.
Rationale
Invite-only, no-directory, sealed-per-Group design is a genuine differentiator that generic « secure! » copy would waste — and naming what the app refuses pre-filters the wrong users (expense-report hunters) before they churn.
Downsides
A register/scope ruling is organizational copy-work beyond the page itself; overplayed it can read as legalistic. Pair it with the voice/content of ideas 3–5 rather than shipping it bare.
